How the source fit works

Every point on a grid over the valley is scored against every report in view: does the reported direction line up with that point, does the reported loudness match how far away that point is, and — for reports with an exact clock time — do the arrival times agree with sound travelling at ~343 m/s from there. The orange cloud is the resulting likelihood; the red pin is its peak.

It is only as good as the reports. Two reports prove nothing. A dozen with careful times and directions will genuinely narrow it down.

Voting. Anyone can report; voting needs an account, because one vote per account costs something and one vote per browser costs nothing. Up means “this matches what I heard”, down means “this looks wrong”. A report that reaches −3 is marked disputed, greyed on the map and dropped from the source fit — so bad data gets filtered by the people who were actually there, not deleted by me.

Where the data comes from. First-hand reports are what people file here, and they are the only thing the fit uses. Chatter is public posts about the booms: a scraper checks r/Bozeman search feeds every 15 minutes and keeps the title, a short quote and a link back, and anyone can paste a link from Facebook, Nextdoor, X or local news. Facebook groups are private and have no API, so they can only arrive by someone pasting a link.

Locations for chatter are read out of the text — a named corner (“Baxter and Davis”) is good to a couple of blocks, “the south side” is good to a mile or so, and posts that name nowhere get no pin at all.

Suspects is just a list of places in the valley that make loud noises for ordinary reasons — a shortlist to rule in or out, not an accusation about any particular boom.
