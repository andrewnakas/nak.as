Getting a legally accurate boundary

The red rectangle on the map is computed from deed acreage, not surveyed. It is right for walking the land and wrong for a dispute. Here is how to replace it with something authoritative, cheapest first.

1 · Call Matthew MacDonald first — he already surveyed this lot

He prepared the 18 July 2016 parcel sketch named in the deed, for G.R. Timber Holdings. His field notes, bearings and any monuments he set are the closest thing to a real survey that already exists. (207) 543‑6365, 135 Star Barn Rd / PO Box 51, Madawaska ME 04756. Ask for a copy of the full sketch and whether he retraced or set corners. This is by far the cheapest path to accuracy.

2 · Pull the recorded plans — they hold the original dimensions

Two documents govern every boundary question here: the 1917 Plan of Eagle Lake, Vol 31 Pg 4, which created the numbered lot tier and fixes Lot 175's 10‑chain width; and Vol 1065 Pg 154, the 1997 Rioux deed. The sketch also cites Book 31 Pg 302 (1913 survey of lots 71–77, 79–86, 122–140, recorded Oct 1915) and Book 14 Pg 406 (1887 Brown plan, lots 50–69). Order them through SearchIQS (MEARN) or in person at Fort Kent — 22 Hall St, Suite 201, (207) 834‑3925, Mon–Fri 8–4. Public terminals are free; copies $1/page.

3 · Order a boundary retracement survey when it has to be defensible

Only a Maine‑licensed surveyor can set legal corners. For a wooded 33‑acre lot with 1917 metes‑and‑bounds calls, expect roughly $3,000–$6,000; firms in the region quote a $3,000 minimum, and Maine is a colonial metes‑and‑bounds state where old descriptions make cost less predictable. You need this before selling, subdividing, building, or contesting a line — and it is the only way to resolve the 30.03 vs 33.69 acre question on the ground.

4 · Free data that sharpens the picture — but is still not a survey

The Maine Office of GIS publishes statewide parcel and LiDAR data, and Aroostook County's assessing parcels can be viewed on the county GIS. LiDAR hillshade often reveals old cut boundary lines, stone walls and woods roads directly. Useful for orientation and for checking my rectangle — but county GIS parcels are assessment approximations and carry an explicit "not for conveyance" disclaimer, exactly like the tax map does.

What would actually make the map on this page exact

Two things, in order: the bearings and distances off MacDonald's sketch, which would let the rectangle be redrawn to true recorded geometry instead of assumed true north; and then GPS coordinates of the found monuments — the cedar post set in 1990, the wood post noted on the sketch — which would pin it to the ground. Standing on a corner with "Show my location" open and reading off the coordinates is a genuinely useful first pass.

Open questions on title

The town still assesses this land to the seller

Eagle Lake's 2024 cards list Map/Lot 11‑006 to G.R. Timber Holdings, LLC, citing deed B1065P154. Only two splits exist: 11‑006‑1 (Michael Schulze, 12 Rioux Road) and 11‑006‑2 (John Ilyin, 33.17 ac). No card in your dad's name, no Lot 175 split. The cards are dated 11/29/2021 so they may predate the sale — but nothing public shows him as owner.

A mortgage deed is not a conveyance

What he holds secures the debt. Now that invoice #1650 reads $0.00 / Paid, the transfer becomes public only once a warranty or quitclaim deed in his name and a discharge of mortgage are recorded at Fort Kent. Confirm both exist.

30.03 vs 33.69 acres

The deed recites 30.03; the invoice bills 30.03 plus 3.66. At 660 ft wide one chain of depth is exactly one acre, so the add‑on is a 242‑ft strip. It must appear in the recorded legal description or it does not legally exist.

Access is entirely by easement

The lot is landlocked. Three 50‑ft rights of way over gravel roads carry access in, and the grantor reserved ROWs across the north and south ends of Lot 175 to reach Lots 174 and 70. Any recorded deed must carry all of these forward.
